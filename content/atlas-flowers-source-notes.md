# Flowers evidence notes

- Treat the visible mature cannabis "bud" as a condensed compound inflorescence made from repeated reproductive phytomers and branch orders, not as one flower.
- Use the 2019 Cannabis florogenesis study and 2024 photoperiod/gibberellin study to separate solitary flower development from short-day-associated shoot-apex branching and internode compression.
- Use the 2026 female reproductive-shoot microscopy from the STS study for control-female anatomy only. Do not transfer STS treatment timing, masculinization timing, or induced-male outcomes into normal female developmental claims.
- Use the 2026 BMC Plant Biology transcriptome study for floral identity and ontogenetic context; transcript abundance is not a direct cultivation prescription.
- Use the 2023 trichome-development study to teach genotype-, tissue-age-, and flower-age-dependent maturation. Its sampled flowering weeks are study conditions, not a universal harvest calendar.
- Keep maturity observation multi-signal: representative bract trichomes, bract expansion, stigma condition, tissue senescence, genotype, canopy position, and disease/mold inspection.
- Do not imply that stigma color directly measures cannabinoid concentration or that every floral tissue matures synchronously.
